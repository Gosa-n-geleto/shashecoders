Rigorous theoretical computer science and systems engineering for secondary scholars in rural Oromia.

ShasheCoders is an intensive, seven-week residential summer program in algorithms, discrete mathematics, and software systems hosted at the Dodola Ifa Boru Special Boarding Secondary School in West Arsi, Oromia, Ethiopia. Inspired by the academic depth of international algorithmic training camps such as AddisCoder, ShasheCoders brings university-level computer science directly to high-potential secondary students from rural woredas and regional public schools.

Founded by Dodola Ifa Boru alumni who achieved the highest scores in the nation on the Ethiopian Secondary School Leaving Examination (592/600 and 590/600) in partnership with computer science faculty at Addis Ababa University, the institute operates on a 4,000,000+ ETB annual budget. Every admitted student attends completely free of charge—receiving full residential boarding, three daily meals, Linux workstation access, a 1:6 teaching assistant mentorship ratio, and a multi-day engineering field excursion across Oromia.

Section I • Pedagogical Architecture

First-Principles Computer Science Grounded in Oromo Collaborative Traditions

Many short-term coding initiatives teach students to copy web frameworks without understanding time complexity, memory layout, or mathematical proof. Conversely, purely competitive environments can alienate students from rural secondary schools who have never touched a personal computer before arrival. ShasheCoders resolves this tension by pairing a rigorous algorithms syllabus with indigenous Oromo mutual-aid frameworks.

1. Jigi (Collective Labor Sprints)

LAB PROTOCOL

In Arsi and Bale agrarian communities, Jigi is the tradition of neighbors uniting to harvest a field that no single household could clear alone. During our four-hour afternoon Linux labs, students form six-person Jigi pods led by a collegiate Teaching Assistant. No scholar moves to the bonus Olympiad problems until every member of their pod has understood and passed the core test suite on their own terminal.

Outcome: 100% cohort retention across all 7 weeks; zero dropouts.

2. Geeddaruu (Reciprocal Exchange)

PEER RECITATION

Scholars arrive with diverse strengths: some have strong competition mathematics backgrounds from boarding school, while others possess sharp practical intuition for systems and hardware. Through Geeddaruu evening recitations, students alternate at the chalkboard—proving recurrence bounds (T(n) = 2T(n/2) + Θ(n)) in both English and Afaan Oromoo so conceptual barriers dissolve immediately.

Outcome: Bilingual algorithmic fluency in English & Afaan Oromoo.

3. Low-Resource Systems

INFRASTRUCTURE

Because regional Ethiopian towns experience intermittent WAN connectivity and grid fluctuations, our Dodola laboratory runs a local intranet judge, offline Python/C++/PostgreSQL documentation mirrors, and dual-grid backup generator power. In Weeks 6–7, students engineer offline-first software tailored for rural clinics, grain cooperatives, and secondary schools.

Outcome: 20 deployed municipal & educational software capstones.
